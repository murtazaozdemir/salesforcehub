Today's alternative is a screenshot

  • Posts get deleted, edited or hidden, often as soon as a dispute starts.
  • A screenshot on its own is easy to fake, so it's easy to challenge. Opposing counsel only has to ask "how do we know this is real?"
  • X Spaces, the live audio rooms, are gone once the broadcast ends, along with who spoke and what the chat said.

When a client calls because someone has just posted about them, capture can start the same day. Once a post is captured, it stays in the record even if the author later deletes it or locks the account. Deleted-post checks then record when posts disappear from X, which matters if spoliation becomes an issue.

What we capture

Posts

  • From named accounts, over a date range, or matching key words
  • Replies, links and media references kept with each post
  • For each account: its permanent user ID, @username, display name and follower count
  • Views, likes, reposts, replies and quotes, as X reported them at capture
  • A picture of each post as X displayed it

X Spaces

  • Title, host, co-hosts, speakers, and start and end times
  • Who was in the audience, and who was brought on stage
  • The room chat and X's live captions
  • The recording, where X kept a replay

The posting date, the fingerprint, the Bitcoin timestamp and the X cross-check can each be repeated by someone else, without trusting us or our software. That is what makes the evidence hard to attack.

What the record proves, and what it leaves to you

The record proves what an account posted, when, and that the copy hasn't changed since. Showing who was behind the account remains part of your case, through the content itself, testimony or a subpoena to X. The preserved account details give you material to build that on.
